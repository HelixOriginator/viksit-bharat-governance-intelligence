The Problem

Flagship welfare schemes leak.
Current monitoring systems rarely diagnose implementation causality.

India's central schemes are designed with intent but face implementation friction at the district, block, and gram panchayat level. Current monitoring systems track outputs, not failure causality. There is no intelligence layer that maps the full chain: policy intent → field friction → citizen impact → administrative correction.

Scheme Leakage

Benefits fail to reach intended citizens due to eligibility gaps, duplication, bureaucratic bottlenecks, and last-mile delivery failures. Root-cause tracing is absent.

Rights Invisibility

Citizens do not know what they are entitled to. Grievance systems capture complaints but do not map systemic patterns of rights denial across schemes and geographies.

Knowledge-System Disconnect

Indian public systems frameworks remain archived but not operationalized for modern implementation analytics and ground-level correction.

Methodology & Evidence

Diagnostic approach & pilot outputs

Transparent methodology, structured data sourcing, and open limitations for institutional review.

Data Sources & Methodology

Primary Data Inputs

Open government portals (PM-Kisan beneficiary lists, grievance redressal platforms, district administration reports, DBT transaction logs, and publicly available census/demographic overlays).

Analytical Method

Cross-dataset reconciliation to identify exclusion/leakage patterns. Friction mapping uses spatial-temporal clustering of payment delays, grievance spikes, and eligibility mismatches at block/district levels. Outputs are structured as diagnostic reports, not predictive models.

Date of Analysis

Pilot baseline compiled: October 2025. Ongoing refresh: Monthly (public dataset dependent).

Limitations

  • • Relies on publicly available administrative data; real-time DBT feeds require institutional API access.
  • • District-level patterns indicate systemic friction but do not replace ground-level audit or beneficiary verification.
  • • Currently optimized for cash-transfer schemes; adaptation to in-kind service delivery requires additional data structuring.

Institutional Feedback Sought

  • • Validation of diagnostic logic by NITI Aayog / Ministry of Finance implementation teams.
  • • Access to anonymized, scheme-level transaction data for friction model calibration.
  • • Guidance on integration pathways with existing DBT / grievance redressal architectures.
